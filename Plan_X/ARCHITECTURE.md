# Architecture

What the system is, where the one intervention happens, and what judges it.
The diagram is `graphs/21_architecture.svg`, generated by
`plot_architecture.py` so it can be corrected when the code moves.

---

## The shape of it in one paragraph

A voice is cloned by CosyVoice2. Inside that clone, between the flow decoder
and the vocoder, there is a mel spectrogram. Everything this project does is
an edit to that one tensor. Four anti-spoofing detectors then say how human
the resulting audio sounds to them, a speaker embedding and a transcript say
whether it is still the right person saying the right words, and three
reference-free quality measures say what the edit cost. The whole project is
the question of whether an edit exists that moves the first number without
moving the other three.

---

## 1. The generator, and the one place we touch it

`cloners.py` loads CosyVoice2. Its pipeline is

    text + reference  ->  LLM  ->  speech tokens  ->  flow decoder
                      ->  mel (80 x T)  ->  HiFT vocoder  ->  wav 24 kHz

The mel is the intervention point, and the mechanism is
`mel_policy.mel_edit_hook`, which monkeypatches `model.hift.inference` for the
duration of a `with` block. Every call inside the block has its `speech_feat`
replaced by `speech_feat + delta` before the real vocoder runs, and the hook
records the edit, its log-probability and the pre-edit mel into a
`HookRecord`.

Two reasons this is the right place and not an earlier or later one:

- **Earlier** is discrete. Speech tokens are categorical, so there is no
  gradient and no small perturbation.
- **Later** is the waveform, where an edit is audible immediately and has no
  structure to exploit.

The mel is continuous, differentiable all the way to the detectors through
HiFT, and perceptually forgiving in the places where speech is loud.

The edit is bounded by construction, `delta = max_delta * tanh(raw)`, rather
than by projection after the fact.

## 2. Two ways to produce an edit, and they do not share a mechanism

This is the division the diagram draws in two colours, and it is the most
important thing to understand about the codebase.

### The white-box path (yellow): gradients

`diffscore.py` makes the whole chain differentiable. Two tricks make it work:

- `hift_forward_fn` reaches past `inference_mode` by taking
  `getattr(fn, "__wrapped__", fn)`, because the decorated version refuses to
  build a graph.
- `differentiable()` turns cuDNN off for the block, because RawNet2's GRU
  cannot backpropagate in eval mode with cuDNN on.

With that, `collect_pgd.py` runs projected gradient descent per clip against
the detectors directly. It is expensive, it is strong, and **it never uses
the reward function at all**. `distill.py` then trains a one-pass student to
imitate the teacher's edits, which is what makes the attack cheap enough to
deploy.

### The reinforcement-learning path (green): one scalar

`reward.py` collapses everything into a single number and the trainers
optimise that. Design A (`train_ppo_mel.py`) is PPO with Lagrangian
constraints; Design B (`train_sac_mel.py`) is SAC with twin critics and the
detector gradient supplied as extra input channels. Both drive the same
`MelResidualPolicy` through the same hook.

The policy is the same object in both paths. The difference is entirely in
what information reaches it: a full gradient, or a scalar.

## 3. What judges a clip

| | file | what it gives | role |
|---|---|---|---|
| detectors | `detectors.py` | AASIST, AASIST-L, RawNet2, RawGAT-ST, each P(human) | the objective |
| aggregate | `reward.py`, `diffscore.py` | soft-min over the four, blended 0.5 / 0.5 with the mean | what is optimised |
| identity | `Tester.py` | CAM++ 192-d embedding, cosine against the reference | constraint, >= 0.70 |
| intelligibility | `Tester.py` | Whisper 'base' transcript, WER through jiwer | constraint, <= 0.10 |
| audio quality | `quality.py` | TorchAudio-SQUIM: PESQ, STOI, SI-SDR, estimated MOS | reported, never optimised |

Soft-min rather than the plain minimum because the minimum has gradient only
for the single worst detector; the blend keeps the already-passing ones
contributing. The quality measures are deliberately outside the objective: an
optimiser given PESQ to maximise finds ways to score well that do not sound
better.

`scorecard.score_clip` is the single call that returns all of it for one
file, and it never raises; anything it could not compute is simply absent
from the result and listed under `problems`.

## 4. Two environments, and why the detectors appear twice

The detector repos and CosyVoice2 pin incompatible stacks:

| | voicefake | voiceclone |
|---|---|---|
| torch | 2.6.0+cu124 | 2.3.1+cu121 |
| numpy | 2.2.6 | 1.26.4 |

The diagram shows the detectors in two places because both paths are real:

- **In process.** The white-box path needs CosyVoice2 and the detectors in
  one graph, so it runs them together under voiceclone. Measured: the two
  environments agree on detector scores to about 1e-7, and voiceclone
  reproduces the stored values exactly.
- **Through a subprocess.** `reward.py` shells out to `VOICEFAKE_PYTHON`
  running `detectors.py --json`, batching a whole rollout into one call
  because loading the four models costs about 13 seconds and dominates a
  single-file call.

The subprocess bridge is therefore a performance and isolation decision for
the RL loop, not a hard requirement of the detectors.

## 5. What comes out

- `ahjn.py` is the front door: `check`, `detect`, `score`, `rate`, `train`,
  `status`, `speak`, `figures`, `verify`.
- `rl/` wraps the two designs with a stable interface: `train`, `summarise`,
  `latest_checkpoint`, `generate`.
- `rl/mos_corpus.py` renders the listening-test corpus, three arms per item
  from one shared seed so the edit is the only audible difference, and the
  rating page collects MOS and PMOS from a person.
- `plot_*.py` write every figure to `graphs/`.
- The write-up lives in `Plan_X/RESULTS.md`, `Plan_X/SOURCES.md`,
  `rl/ANALYSIS.md` and `MATH.md`, and `verify_claims.py` re-derives the
  headline numbers from stored JSON.

## 6. The one invariant worth protecting

**Paired, same-seed evaluation.** Both policies are deterministic at
inference and consume no RNG, so for a given seed the edited and unedited
arms replay the same speech tokens and the same vocoder noise. Without that,
two clips of the same line are different takes and nothing measured about
them is comparable. Every evaluation script in the repo depends on it, and
the rendered files coming out identical in length is the check that it held.
